# Decision Matrix

Who decides what? Score each decision on two questions: **can it be undone easily?** (a two-way door or a one-way door) and **how much trouble if it's wrong?** Where it lands says what to do.

```
                       trouble if wrong:  SMALL           BIG
 easy to undo (two-way door)    | GO: decide, then | PROPOSE: two options,
                                | tell in the      | your pick and why; go
                                | stand-up         | ahead unless someone
                                |                  | objects by an agreed time
 hard to undo (one-way door)    | PROPOSE          | SIGN-OFF: the owner
                                |                  | decides
```

Examples:

- **Go:** offering a guest a time slot; fixing a typo on a live page; choosing which inbox item to do first.
- **Propose:** picking a tool under a small monthly cost; changing a recurring procedure; a public post in a voice already agreed.
- **Sign-off:** a new contract or supplier; spending above an agreed limit; a public statement in the organisation's name.

How to propose: two options, your pick, one line of reasoning, and the time after which you'll go ahead. This is level 4 in [[Five levels of agency]]; "go, then tell" is level 5.

The line moves. Agree per area where it sits, and widen it on purpose as trust and track record grow (see [[Self-scoring and Review]]).

Credit: the one-way / two-way door idea is Jeff Bezos's (Amazon shareholder letter, 2015).
