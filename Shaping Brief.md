# Shaping Brief

Well-defined work increasingly goes to AI. What reaches a person is often loosely defined on purpose: "sort out X", "get Y moving". Turning that into a piece of work with a goal, a timebox and a decision is **shaping**, and it is a skill you can practise.

Whoever asks defines the **outcome, not the method**. Whoever does the work writes a short brief before starting:

1. **Outcome:** what "done" looks like, plus a test you can check ([[Definition of Done]]).
2. **Why:** one or two lines of context. What's the situation, and what's changed?
3. **Options:** two ways to do it, your pick and why.
4. **Timebox:** how long it's worth, and when you'll check in.
5. **Decision rights:** where it sits in the [[Decision Matrix]]: go, propose or sign-off.
6. **Score:** how you'll score the result (see [[Self-scoring and Review]]).

Shaping and doing are reviewed separately. A quick review of the brief catches most rework before it happens; the doing then needs less checking. An AI critique of the brief against the area's "what good looks like" can come before any human review.

**Bigger or newer work** (a product, a new area, a team process) needs more than a brief: use the `project-planning` skill in [life-itself/skills](https://github.com/life-itself/skills/tree/main/skills/project-planning) (situation, complication, question and an issue tree, then a Logical Thinking Process). The brief is the light version of the same thinking.
