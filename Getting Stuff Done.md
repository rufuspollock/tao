- [Job Stories](https://rufuspollock.com/tao/Job%20Stories) for understanding what people actually need.

- [Getting Things Done (David Allen)](https://rufuspollock.com/post/getting-things-done) for personal task management.
- "Shape and Ship" for how larger work moves from raw idea to shipped.

## Shape and Ship

Work passes through four stages: idea → shaped pitch → selected ("bet on") → shipped. There's selection at every stage -- only some raw ideas get shaped, and only some shaped ideas get bet on. That filtering is the point.

**Shaping** means clearly framing the problem, and the shape of a solution, before you commit to building it. The output is a short pitch: situation, problem, solution, appetite. **Selection** ("betting") is choosing which shaped pitches you'll commit to for the next cycle. **Shipping** is doing the work, with minimal distraction, inside a fixed appetite.

In practice this runs in cycles: roughly two weeks of shaping and selecting (also a cool-down to tie up loose ends and clear inboxes), followed by roughly two weeks of focused shipping where each person works on one project only.

A few things make it work:

- **Fix the appetite, not the features** -- decide how much time something deserves and cut scope to fit, rather than letting scope dictate the timeline.
- **Protect the shipping window** -- once you've committed, minimise interrupts and context-switching.
- **Respect the scope** -- avoid rabbit holes and no-gos once you've bet on something.
- **Don't let overruns become the norm** -- if it's not ready to ship, ship what you have and let the rest go back through shaping next cycle.
