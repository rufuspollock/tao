# Meetings Prep
Pairs with [[Meetings]] (which covers structure and patterns during the meeting itself) -- this page is specifically about how I prepare for and capture a meeting:
- **Keep meeting notes in one place, usually Google Docs.** One doc per recurring meeting or topic, not scattered across tools -- easy to search back through later and easy to share. See [[Meetings]] for the fuller note-taking pattern.
- **Prepare your questions before the meeting, not during it.** Walk in with your questions already framed so the time gets spent on answers and discussion, not on working out what to ask.
- **Record the meeting.** You don't have to catch everything live if it's captured -- you can go back and check exactly what was said, not just what you remember.
- **Get it transcribed.** Turn the recording into searchable text and a rough summary, so you're not the only person who can process what happened afterward -- plenty of tools do this well (we currently like [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/), but don't take that as gospel since tools change fast).
Preparing questions in advance does the same job as [Hypotheses](https://tao.rufuspollock.com/Hypotheses): it forces you to think first, so the meeting sharpens your thinking rather than replacing it.
